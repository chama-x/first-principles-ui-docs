OKLCH Uniformity Across Hues

Interactive Drills

When user interface surfaces use raw, high-chroma colors, they trigger intense visual vibration. Large saturated cards demand excessive cognitive attention, overwhelming surrounding data and inducing rapid retinal fatigue.

The substrate foundation is the harsh neon glare:

  1. Perceived Luminance Surge: Saturated colors appear physically brighter to the human eye than neutral gray surfaces of identical physical photometric luminance.
  2. Visual Clamor: When applied to large background areas (> 2400px²), high chroma disrupts ocular fixation, causing foreground text to vibrate against the background boundary.
  3. Territorial Budget: High chroma (Radix Step 9) is designed for tiny accents, dots, and active icons. Spreading it across substantial viewport fractions destroys informational hierarchy.

In standard HSL or sRGB, changing hue at constant saturation causes massive perceived lightness fluctuations (pure yellow appears blindingly bright while pure blue appears dark). OKLCH decouples lightness ($L$) and chroma ($C$) so colors transition uniformly:

Remediation

Use calibrated Radix Step 3–5 surface fills or reduce OKLCH chroma:

/* Right: Subtle, elegant surface chroma */
.surface-card {
  background-color: oklch(0.22 0.03 260); /* Low chroma, non-vibrating */
  border: 1px solid oklch(0.30 0.04 260);
}

What to Look For & How the Linter Fixes It

Visual signs in your UI and how the verifier guides your coding agent.

Visual Symptom
  • A vibrant neon card or sidebar overpowers every metric and chart on the screen.
  • Dark mode surfaces cause eye strain and visual vibration around high-chroma cards.
  • Accent colors appear dramatically brighter or harsher when changing hue.
Linter Check
  • Large surface (> 2400px2) with OKLCH chroma C > 0.195 (dark) or C > 0.220 (light).
  • Cumulative high-chroma area exceeds 8% of total viewport area.
